Lalita and Bhandasura

Return to page

The Brahmanda Purana, Part IV. Translated and annotated by Dr. G. V. Tagare. Motilal Banarsidass. Lalita Mahatmya, chapters 11–18 and 26–30. Printed pages 1073–1106 and 1168–1221.

The chapter-and-verse references below identify the main passages used. The commanders’ episode is condensed; the intervening campaign chapters contain additional battles and divine figures.

Passages behind the key moments+
  • Bhanda’s origin: 11.25–38.
  • Lalita’s manifestation and promise: 12.59–75; 13.29–36.
  • The royal union and march: chapters 14–18.
  • Bala: 26.72–117.
  • Ganesha and the obstacle: 27.24–76; 27.102–104.
  • The commanders: chapter 28.
  • The final duel: 29.55–144.
  • Recovery and Kama’s restoration: 29.145–146; 30.4–6; 30.31–56.

Names are written in a simplified spelling for readability. Bhanda and Bhandasura refer to the same figure; Kama, Madana and Manmatha name the god of love. Mantrini is also called Shyamala, and Dandanatha is also called Dandini.

Images are devotional interpretations accompanying the story. Devotional artworks retain their visible artist or website marks; the Ganesha illustration is AI-generated.

Explore the Puranas

Tagare’s notes to chapter 29, printed pages 1202–1203, interpret the opposing missiles as harmful and beneficial forces. The narrative itself describes divine weapons and their effects.

# Narada source review

Primary source: owner-supplied Gita Press Sanskrit–Hindi *Srimad Bhagavat Mahapuran*, Volume 1, locally retained as searchable OCR in `.qa/creation-sources/bhagavata-volume1-ocr.txt`. The public site links the same supplied edition on Internet Archive; it does not distribute the private scan or reproduce its Hindi explanation.

The narrative spine is Narada’s own account to Vyasa in Bhagavata Purana 1.5–6: Narada’s counsel to Vyasa (1.5.1–22); his former life as the child of a maidservant, service to travelling sages, listening and instruction (1.5.23–31); his mother’s death, northward journey, meditation and brief divine encounter (1.6.1–25); and his later emergence, travelling life and vina-centred praise (1.6.26–39).

Selected later deeds are presented as separate appearances rather than a continuous biography: guidance to Dhruva (4.8.25–63) and Prahlada’s recollection of instruction heard before birth (7.7.1–55). Those verse ranges and narrative boundaries were already verified for the existing Dhruva and Prahlada readers.

Editorial boundaries: the page does not claim that every account of Narada forms one chronological life. It does not use the Narada Purana as the source of Narada’s birth narrative; that Mahapurana is a distinct text named for him. The mother’s death is stated without graphic detail. Direct speech and extended teachings are condensed into original English prose. The closing reflection and the “guidance without possession” formulation are editorial.

Images are newly generated devotional interpretations and are not historical reconstructions. Faces, clothing, hermitage, landscape and celestial path are imagined. All three masters are exactly 1600×900 (16:9), and each is used once.

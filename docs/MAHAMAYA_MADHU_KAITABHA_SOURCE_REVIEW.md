# Mahamaya, Madhu and Kaitabha source review

## Supplied edition

The reader follows the owner-supplied F. E. Pargiter English translation of the *Markandeya Purana* (1904), Canto 81, printed pages 465–472, supplied PDF pages 511–518. This canto is explicitly headed as the commencement of the Devi Mahatmya and the slaughter of Madhu and Kaitabha.

The source PDF remains outside `src`, `dist` and GitHub. Pages 511–518 were extracted and visually rendered in `.qa/mahamaya-sources/` to confirm the text and pagination.

## Narrative scope

- 81.1–28: Suratha and Samadhi reach Medhas’s hermitage and describe attachment that persists despite understanding.
- 81.29–46: Medhas explains Mahamaya as both deluding power and the source of liberating knowledge.
- 81.47–53: the eternal Goddess, cosmic ocean, Vishnu’s sleep and the threat to Brahma.
- 81.54–67: Brahma’s hymn and request that the Goddess awaken Vishnu and delude the asuras.
- 81.68–77: Mahamaya withdraws, Vishnu awakens, battles the pair and uses their offered boon to overcome them.

## Editorial decisions

- The philosophical frame is retained because it explains why Medhas tells the battle narrative.
- Mahamaya is not reduced to “illusion” in the everyday sense; the passage also names her supreme knowledge, world-power and cause of liberation.
- Mahamaya awakens and deludes; Vishnu fights and defeats Madhu and Kaitabha. The roles are not merged.
- The combat is condensed and non-graphic.
- The pause-and-reflect question is clearly editorial.

## Art review

Three images were commissioned using built-in Imagegen. The hero shows Mahamaya withdrawing from Vishnu’s sleep, Brahma on the lotus and the two asuras in the distance. The inline painting gives Vishnu four Vaishnava attributes and shows two distinct, two-armed opponents beneath Mahamaya’s symbolic presence. The square card is a symbolic still life.

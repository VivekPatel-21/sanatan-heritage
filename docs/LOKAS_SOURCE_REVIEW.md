# Fourteen Lokas: verified owner-supplied source research

Read-only research, 25 October 2026. Use the existing exact owner-supplied Gita Press Bhagavata Volume 1 link already cited on Sources. No substitute editions or web summaries were used. The file `.qa/creation-sources/bhagavata-volume1.pdf` has 1,617 reflowed ebook pages; PDF positions below are NOT original printed pages. The accompanying Sanskrit and Hindi explanation were both checked. All listed relevant pages were rendered to PNG and visually inspected. These private scans and QA extracts must not be published.

## Strongest basis: the fourteen names

**Bhagavata Purana 2.5.36-42, reflow PDF pp.350-351.** Verse36 explicitly speaks of seven below and seven above as contemplative placements in the cosmic person's limbs. This is not just a modern secondary list. Verse38-39 lists the upper seven; verses40-41 the lower seven. Verse42 gives an alternative three-world placement, so do not claim a single invariant body map across every passage.

Upper order, from earth upward:
1. Bhuloka / Bhur (earth): feet, 2.5.38.
2. Bhuvarloka / Bhuvar: navel, 2.5.38.
3. Svarloka / Svar (also use familiar Svarga as reader-facing variant only if clearly associated): heart, 2.5.38.
4. Maharloka / Mahar: chest, 2.5.38.
5. Janaloka / Jana: neck, 2.5.39.
6. Tapoloka / Tapa: breasts, 2.5.39.
7. Satyaloka / Satya: head, explicitly also called Brahmaloka, 2.5.39; accompanying explanation identifies Brahma's abode.

Lower order, descending from earth: **Atala, Vitala, Sutala, Talatala, Mahatala, Rasatala, Patala**, 2.5.40-41. Sanskrit matches these names. Prefer this spelling order to silently importing variant lower-seven lists from other texts.

**Bhagavata 2.6.18-21, reflow PDF354-355**: accompanying explanation explicitly places Mahar above Bhur/Bhuvar/Svar and Jana/Tapa/Satya above Mahar. Jana/Tapa/Satya collectively associated with brahmacarins, forest-dwellers and renunciants. Do NOT silently assign one group per realm. Verse21 says the divine source both pervades and transcends the cosmos; the map need not exhaust divinity.

## Lower seven: specific descriptions

**Bhagavata 5.24.7-13, reflow PDF1077-1079**: below earth are seven bhu-vivaras; listed in exactly the above order. Verse8 calls them bilasvargas, with palaces, gardens, enjoyment and inhabitants including Daityas, Danavas, Nagas. Verses9-13 elaborate architecture, gardens, jewel-light and lack of ordinary sunlight/day-night divisions. A dark cavern photograph or a punishment-only description would misrepresent this passage.

- **Atala:** Bala, son of Maya; 5.24.16, PDF1079-1080. The verse includes erotic/intoxicant details unnecessary for a general introductory page; use concise identity instead of sensationalism.
- **Vitala:** Shiva as Hatakeshvara, with Bhavani; 5.24.17, PDF1079-1080 (Sanskrit begins bottom1079, explanation1080). Golden river imagery belongs to this account; no need detailed causal paraphrase.
- **Sutala:** Bali lives there by divine grace; 5.24.18, PDF1080-1081. Narayana at Bali's gate in5.24.27, PDF1081-1082. Verse19 distinguishes Bali's passing wealth from the more profound fruit of devotion; do not call this realm itself moksha.
- **Talatala:** Maya, connected with Tripura, protected by Mahadeva; 5.24.28, PDF1081-1082.
- **Mahatala:** many-headed serpents descended from Kadru; 5.24.29, PDF1082-1083.
- **Rasatala:** Daityas and Danavas; 5.24.30, PDF1082-1083.
- **Patala:** Naga lords led by Vasuki, their hood jewels illuminating the realm; 5.24.31, PDF1082-1083.

## Distinguish lower realms from Naraka

**Bhagavata 5.26.4-6, PDF1089-1090** separately asks where Narakas are and places them in its cosmology, with Yama administering karmic consequences. Verse7 enumerates21 or28 Narakas. This is a separate passage/category from the seven bilasvargas of5.24. Avoid treating Patala as a direct synonym of Christian hell or all seven lower realms as seven punishment levels. The strong claim supported here is simply that the supplied Bhagavata describes these categories separately.

## Upper realms and liberation

**Bhagavata 2.2.25-28, PDF330-331**: Maharloka revered by knowers of Brahman; yogic ascent proceeds onward at cosmicfire to Brahmaloka, whose lifetime is Brahma's. Even after Satyaloka is reached, verse28 and following describe passing the cosmic coverings. Thus avoid casually identifying highest listed world with final liberation. More detailed account of moksha would require full chapter context.

**3.11.29, PDF481**, previously reviewed in creation research: Bhrigu and others move from Maharloka to Janaloka when afflicted by the heat during withdrawal. Useful concise statement that realms participate in differing rhythms of cosmic time, with an exact example; do not describe every realm as simultaneously destroyed every night.

## Editorial scope / limitations

Distinguish traditional cosmological and contemplative presentation from modern astronomical location claims. Do not convert yojana measures to kilometres or label these as observed exoplanets. ## Additional verified contemplative framing

**2.1.25-30, PDF320-321**, rendered and visually inspected: the cosmic person is an object of dharana/contemplation. Its body-world correspondences differ from2.5 (e.g. Svar chest, Mahar neck, Jana mouth, Tapa forehead, Satya head in2.1.28). Therefore never combine the two passages' body maps as though identical. The sky/atmospheric expanse is called nabhas/nabhastala at the navel in2.1.27, and the earth is bhutala at the pelvis. The accessible exposition may identify the upper seven by names/order while explaining that contemplative body images are another textual register, with exact passage attribution if used.

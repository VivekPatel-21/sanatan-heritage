## Passage map

- 9.4.15–23, PDF pp. 31–33: Ambarisha’s devotion and use of the senses in service.
- 9.4.24–35, pp. 33–35: the year-long vow, concluding worship and Durvasa’s arrival.
- 9.4.36–42, pp. 35–36: the Yamuna, Dvadashi deadline, consultation and water.
- 9.4.43–50, pp. 36–37: anger, the fiery being and Sudarshana’s intervention.
- 9.4.51–59, pp. 37–39: pursuit and the appeals to Brahma and Shiva.
- 9.4.60–71, pp. 39–41: Vishnu’s teaching and the return to Ambarisha.
- 9.5.1–13, pp. 42–44: Ambarisha’s prayer to Sudarshana.
- 9.5.14–24, pp. 44–46: reconciliation, hospitality and the year of waiting.

## Editorial boundaries

- The reader is an original condensed retelling, not a translation.
- The conflict of duties around Dvadashi and hospitality is retained rather than reduced to a generic lesson about fasting.
- Vishnu’s bond with devotees is presented within the Bhagavata’s Vaishnava theology.
- Durvasa’s anger is not treated as a timeless caricature of the sage; the story includes his fear, appeal and recognition.
- Ambarisha’s prayer for Durvasa is kept central to the resolution.
- The reflection on protection without humiliation is editorial.

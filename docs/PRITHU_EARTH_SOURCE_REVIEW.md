# King Prithu and Mother Earth — source review

## Supplied edition

The public reader follows the owner-supplied `Vishnu Purana.pdf`: Gita Press Sanskrit–Hindi illustrated edition, first part, chapter 13, private PDF pages 57–63 and printed pages 58–64. The contents title is “Raja Vena aur Prithu ka Charitra.” The scan itself remains outside the repository and public build.

## Narrative boundary

- Vena’s rejection of sacred and social responsibility precedes disorder in the realm.
- The sages bring forth Nishada and then Prithu from Vena’s body; Prithu receives royal attributes and consecration.
- Prithu refuses praise for deeds not yet performed. The bards instead describe virtues toward which he should govern.
- The starving people appeal to Prithu. Earth takes cow form and flees; Prithu pursues her.
- Earth argues that killing her would remove the support of all beings. Prithu answers from his responsibility to protect the many.
- Earth proposes an alternative: level the ground, choose a calf and vessel, and receive what has been hidden.
- Prithu levels the ground, appoints Swayambhuva Manu as calf, uses his hand as the vessel, and receives seeds and medicines.
- Earth is treated as Prithu’s daughter and receives the name Prithvi in the supplied account.

## Editorial safeguards

- Stewardship and accountable prosperity are marked as contemporary editorial reflection, not translated verses or a claim that the Purana is a modern policy document.
- The story does not celebrate Prithu’s threat as the solution; it centres the dialogue and agreement that follow it.
- The wider Puranic catalogues of different beings milking Earth are not merged into this Vishnu Purana retelling.
- Generated images are labelled as devotional or symbolic interpretations. Their clothing, landscape and settlement details are not historical evidence.

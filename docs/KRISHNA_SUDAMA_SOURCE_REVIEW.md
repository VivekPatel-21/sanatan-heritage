# Krishna and Sudama source review

Primary source: owner-supplied Gita Press Sanskrit–Hindi *Srimad Bhagavat Mahapuran*, Volume 2, Skandha 10, chapters 80–81. The locally retained private research copy is `.qa/avadhuta-volume2.pdf`; the story occupies PDF pp. 919–933. Those page positions belong to a 1,520-page reflowed ebook and are not asserted as the printed Gita Press pagination.

The chapter openings and conclusion were rendered and visually inspected. OCR was used only for locating and close reading, with the rendered pages controlling headings and verse sequence. No web summary or alternate retelling was substituted.

## Narrative decisions

- The source calls the visitor Krishna’s learned brahmana friend; the Hindi chapter headings name Sudama. The reader uses the familiar name while retaining that textual distinction.
- The household has little food, but Sudama is characterized by contentment and non-attachment rather than by humiliation.
- Sudama’s wife gathers four handfuls of flattened rice from neighbouring brahmana households and ties them in cloth (10.80.13–15).
- Krishna rises from Rukmini’s couch, embraces Sudama, washes his feet and honours him while Rukmini fans him (10.80.18–26).
- The forest storm and gathered firewood belong to the friends’ shared memory of service in Sandipani’s gurukula (10.80.35–45).
- Sudama does not make a spoken request for wealth. Krishna receives one handful of flattened rice; Rukmini stays his second (10.81.8–11).
- Material abundance appears at Sudama’s home, but the conclusion stresses non-attachment, growing devotion and liberation (10.81.24–41).

## Interpretive limits

The page does not promise wealth in exchange for offerings. “Friendship beyond wealth” is an editorial frame supported by the narrative contrast between palace and poverty, Krishna’s immediate embrace, and Sudama’s lasting wish for friendship and service. It is marked separately from the retelling.

All art is newly generated. Architecture, clothing, faces, the storm landscape and still-life arrangement are contemporary devotional interpretations, not historical reconstructions.

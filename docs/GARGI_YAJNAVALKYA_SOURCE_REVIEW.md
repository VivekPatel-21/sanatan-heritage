## Passage map used

- Brihadaranyaka 3.1.1–2: Janaka’s sacrifice, the thousand cows and Yajnavalkya’s claim.
- 3.6.1: Gargi’s first sequence of cosmological questions and Yajnavalkya’s warning.
- 3.8.1–2: Gargi’s return and the image of two arrows.
- 3.8.3–6: what lies above, below and between; all woven in akasha.
- 3.8.7–9: akasha woven in the Imperishable; cosmic order under the akshara.
- 3.8.10–11: unseen seer and unknown knower; Gargi’s judgment before the assembly.

## Editorial boundaries

- The two Gargi passages are kept distinct; intervening debates are not silently removed from the source history.
- The website text is an original condensed retelling, not a translation.
- Akasha is introduced as space or the vast expanse without claiming that one English word exhausts it.
- Yajnavalkya’s warning in 3.6 is reported without turning it into the conclusion of Gargi’s role.
- Brahman and the Imperishable are not personified in the artwork.
- The court, clothing, physical appearances and woven-sky metaphor are imagined.

# Ajamila source review

## Scope

The reader follows the owner-supplied Gita Press Sanskrit–Hindi *Srimad Bhagavat Mahapuran*, Volume 1, Skandha 6, chapters 1–3. The public page links to the matching Internet Archive scan; the private PDF is not redistributed.

## Checked passages

- 6.1.20–39 (private PDF pp. 1103–1106): old age, Narayana, death scene and arrival of both groups of messengers.
- 6.1.40–68 (pp. 1106–1110): debate about dharma and Ajamila’s life history.
- 6.2.1–19 (pp. 1112–1115): divine name, atonement and cleansing the source of conduct.
- 6.2.20–38 (pp. 1115–1118): release, remorse and resolve.
- 6.2.39–49 (pp. 1118–1119): Gangadvara, later practice and conclusion.
- 6.3.1–18 (pp. 1126–1129): Yamadutas question Yama; Yama’s reply.
- 6.3.19–35 (pp. 1129–1133): Yama’s instruction on devotion and the name.

The chapter openings and narrative pages were rendered and visually inspected in `.qa/ajamila-sources/` to check OCR-derived locations against the scan.

## Editorial decisions

- The story includes the full post-rescue arc. It does not claim that an accidental utterance makes prior harm irrelevant.
- Responsibility remains with Ajamila. The partner he joins is not presented as the cause of his choices.
- Young Narayana is presented as an innocent child, not as a device or miniature divine figure.
- “Gangadvara, now associated with Haridwar” is used as orientation without pretending to reconstruct a precise ancient location.
- The reflection questions are clearly marked as editorial.

## Art review

The hero, Gangadvara scene and card image were commissioned with built-in Imagegen. The hero uses cords and sacred emblems symbolically and avoids horror. The meditation image conveys time, discipline and a redirected life. The card uses a conch, lotus, cords and toy cart without writing or a literal child.

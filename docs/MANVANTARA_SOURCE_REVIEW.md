## Definition and time scale

- **3.11.18–21, PDF479:** Satya/Krita, Treta, Dvapara and Kali comprise the four-yuga cycle. The text discusses their dawn/twilight portions; avoid treating a bare count as though it excluded its own timing qualifications.
- **3.11.22, PDF479–480:** Brahma's day is one thousand four-yuga cycles; night is of equal duration. Source distinguishes day and night rather than calling both together one thousand cycles.
- **3.11.23, PDF479–480:** fourteen Manus exercise their authority during that day/kalpa.
- **3.11.24, PDF480:** each Manu's period is *sadhika* (more than) seventy-one four-yuga cycles. The supplied explanation explicitly shows 71 6/14. Safe introductory prose: “a little more than seventy-one four-yuga cycles.” Do NOT present an exact71-only period without discussing its qualifications. There is no need for human-year conversion or a numerical modern date.
- **3.11.24–26, PDF480:** recurring Manu lineages/royal descendants, seven rishis, deity groups, Indra and attendants; divine power maintains/protects the cosmos through such manifestations.
- **8.13.36, PDF1532:** explicitly describes fourteen manvantaras in past/present/future and the thousand-four-yuga kalpa. Helpful for clear sequence context.

## What changes in a Manvantara: shared sacred responsibilities

**8.14.1–7, PDF1533–1534**, visually inspected:

1. Parikshit asks who appoints the Manu, his sons, the rishis and other figures and what their work is.
2. The divine Purusha appoints the Manus, sons, sages, Indra and deity groups (8.14.2).
3. Manus and others, guided by divine manifestations such as Yajna, maintain the order of the world (8.14.3).
4. The seven rishis restore the Vedic dharma obscured/lost at a four-yuga cycle's end (8.14.4).
5. Each Manu upholds dharma in his own time (8.14.5).
6. Manu's sons divide jurisdictions of space/time and protect people while upholding dharma; deity groups receive sacrificial shares (8.14.6).
7. Indra enjoys/protects the three worlds granted by the divine source and gives rain (8.14.7).

“Cosmic administration” is acceptable modern explanatory shorthand for this coordinated sacred work. Do not attribute modern bureaucratic language directly to the ancient text or reduce dharma to secular administration alone.

## Useful concrete examples, if needed

- **8.13.1–6, PDF1528:** present Vaivasvata, Purandara as Indra, seven rishis and Vamana's manifestation. Keep any names actually listed rather than inventing a universal unchanging seven-rishi list.
- **8.13.11–17, PDF1529–1531:** future Savarni period, Bali as Indra (v12), present preparatory austerities of future sages (v15–16), divine Sarvabhauma aiding transfer of Indra's office (v17). An accessible example is “Bali is named as Indra of the future Savarni period,” with attribution.
- **8.1.19–30, PDF1428–1430** and **8.5.2–10, PDF1453–1454** illustrate past periods with distinct Manus, deity groups, Indras, sages and divine manifestations. No need to reconstruct a complete14-by-many-offices chart.

## Editorial cautions

Keep kalpa/day, manvantara/Manu's period, and four-yuga cycle distinct. Fourteen Manus are not fourteen Lokas. Do not conflate cosmic administration with the absolute source/creator or the final liberation of beings. Do not present cyclical religious time as an observation-based modern astronomical chronology. No precise elapsed-year date or current yuga ordinal is needed. Distinguish primary account and optional editorial reflection. Private source pages, copyrighted translations and book scans must not be uploaded.

# Understanding Sanatan Dharma — source review

## Editorial scope

The guide introduces three related but distinct frameworks: the four Purusharthas, the three Gunas and the six classical Darshanas. It does not present them as one universal ladder or imply that every Hindu tradition interprets them identically.

## Owner-supplied source boundary

- **Gunas:** *Srimad Bhagavat Mahapuran*, Gita Press, Volume 2, 11.25.1–36; compare Volume 1, 3.26.10–18.
- **Purusharthas:** the same edition, 10.80.45, 11.5.35–36 and 11.21.6–7.
- **Nyaya:** the owner-supplied *Nyaya Sutras of Gautama* translation and commentary, 1.1.1–7.
- **Sankhya narrative context:** the supplied Bhagavata edition used for the Kapila and Devahuti reader.

Descriptions of the other Darshanas remain concise orientation rather than claims of exhaustive coverage. The page explicitly distinguishes philosophical Darshanas from broad Shaiva, Vaishnava, Shakta and Smarta traditions.

## Art boundary

The pavilion and handloom images are AI-generated symbolic interpretations created for this page. Four paths, three currents and six gateways are an editorial visualization, not a scriptural diagram. Thread colors do not assign fixed moral identities to the Gunas.

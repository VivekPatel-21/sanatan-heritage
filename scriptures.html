01 / FINDING YOUR BEARINGS

Many texts.
Many ways of learning.

These categories offer a starting map. The authority and interpretation of a text also depend on the tradition reading it.

श्रुति

Shruti

“That which is heard”

The Vedic corpus is revered as revealed knowledge. It includes the Upanishads. [1] [2]

स्मृति

Smriti

“That which is remembered”

A broad category including epics and Puranas, conveying teachings through narrative and reflection. [1]

What is inside the Vedic corpus?+

A common introduction distinguishes four kinds of literature: Samhitas (hymns and mantras), Brahmanas (ritual explanations), Aranyakas (forest treatises) and Upanishads (spiritual and philosophical teachings). The Vedas contain more than hymns alone. [2]

ACTION / KNOWLEDGE / DEVOTION

The Bhagavad Gita

Krishna’s dialogue with Arjuna on spiritual life amid a difficult decision. [5]

Look closer+

The Gita belongs to the Mahabharata. It is often read on its own, with commentaries that offer different understandings of its teachings. [1]

ITIHASA / THE GREAT EPICS

Ramayana & Mahabharata

Epic journeys through devotion, duty, family and conflict. [1]

Look closer+

The Ramayana follows Rama’s life and trials. The Mahabharata centres on a conflict between related families and explores difficult questions of dharma. Retellings bring distinct voices: Valmiki’s Ramayana and Tulsidas’s Ramcharitmanas are different works.

DEITIES / COSMOS / SACRED STORIES

The Puranas

Collections of sacred narratives that express teachings through stories. [1]

Look closer+

Tradition commonly names eighteen major Puranas, alongside many other Puranic works. This is a classification, not a count of all Hindu scriptures. Each story identifies the text and version it draws from.

04 / TEXTS & LIVING PRACTICE

Agamas
& Tantras.

These bodies of literature connect philosophy with worship, meditation, mantra, temple building and sacred imagery. Their teachings and authority are understood within particular lineages. [4]

Shaiva, Vaishnava and Shakta traditions have their own textual heritages. These names cover diverse works rather than a single book or one uniform teaching.

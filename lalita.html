01 / THE DIVINE MOTHER

Beyond a name.
A vision of the Divine.

In the Lalita Mahatmya, she is praised as the source, support and resting place of the universe.

The devas address Lalita as the Mother of all, while also praising her as the reality beyond form and beyond the reach of words. The narrative brings together a radiant personal presence and an ultimate reality that cannot be contained in an image.

This is a Shakta portrayal of the supreme Goddess. Other Hindu traditions explain divine relationships from their own perspectives.

02 / HER DIVINE FORM

Four emblems.
A radiant presence.

At her manifestation, the text describes Lalita with the lustre of the rising sun, red garments and a merciful glance.

03

One of the four emblems held in her hands.

04

Ankusha · the goad

The goad appears both in her divine form and in the army narrative, where Sampatkari arises from it.

04 / SRI CHAKRA

A sacred diagram.
An inward doorway.

Sri Chakra is also called Sri Yantra. The ArunaLahari commentary on the Bhavanopanishad connects its contemplation with the body and the Self.

From outer form
to inner contemplation.

The ArunaLahari commentary distinguishes external worship, internal worship and bhavana, or contemplative understanding. Its introduction relates the yantra and mantra to the body itself, and directs attention towards the Self as not different from Devi.

In the Brahmanda Purana’s hymn, Lalita is praised in relation to the bindu, the central point. The story and the contemplative text offer different entrances into her sacred presence.

05 / A DOORWAY INTO SRI VIDYA

Devotion.
Contemplation.
Knowledge.

An introduction to a tradition centred on Lalita, sacred mantra and Sri Chakra.

These subjects meet in the Bhavanopanishad commentary’s account of worship and the recognition of the Self. The commentary also gives an important place to the teacher in understanding the text. It presents a particular traditional interpretation, rather than a single explanation shared by every lineage.

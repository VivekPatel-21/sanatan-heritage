# Creative ideas — optional, not approved implementation

Codex should recommend thoughtfully, explain the reader benefit and ask approval before changing an approved design. Avoid adding everything at once.

1. **A source-aware journey into each story.** Offer a compact “read the story / explore its deity / understand its terms” connection near the conclusion. Benefit: useful discovery rather than generic related cards. First assess the links already present.
2. **Curated beginner routes that are honest about completeness.** A short path with finished readers is more useful than a large route containing unavailable chapters. Propose any labels/content changes for approval.
3. **An illustrated family companion.** A separate printable page with a few faithful story beats and a discussion question. Reuse no visible website art; commission/create a distinct print illustration if needed.
4. **Pronunciation as a listening aid.** A small optional audio pronunciation control for glossary terms after linguistic review. No autoplay; keep text and keyboard use complete.
5. **A calm narration reader.** Offer play/pause, transcript and chapter markers when approved audio exists. Preserve the editorial page layout rather than adding a visually dominant player.
6. **Editorial image commissions with a provenance ledger.** For each future story, plan distinct cover/card/interior subjects and record source, permission, symbolism and rejection reasons. This supports the owner's no-repeat policy and traditional accuracy.
7. **A source comparison companion.** Where accounts differ, a concise optional comparison on the Sources page can clarify tradition rather than blending versions. Requires textual review and owner content approval.
8. **Festival-to-story connections.** Explain why a narrative is associated with a festival before attempting a dynamic tithi calendar. This is useful without prematurely adding date calculations.
9. **A text-first sacred places guide.** Begin with verified location entries and story links; add an accessible map only if it improves navigation. Confirm the owner's temple photographs before labelling them.
10. **Measured professional polish.** Use rendered evidence to prioritise typography wrapping, image crops, spacing, responsive nav and reading comfort. Small improvements within an approved scope can matter more than another wholesale aesthetic change.

Do not treat these as website copy or public promises. The existing ivory/obsidian-and-gold system, Cormorant Garamond/Manrope and approved information remain the baseline.
